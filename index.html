Strategy foundation

Locked

Defined and backtested the core edge: a 5-minute opening-range breakout with a confirmation entry, tested across an 883k-row trade history to lock in parameters rather than eyeball them.

Stop: 0.25× opening range
Target: 5.0× risk
Entry: confirmed breakout, not first tick
Validation: split-half tested before adoption

Signal quality gates

Live

Added gates so the strategy only fires when conditions historically supported it — including a counter-intuitive finding that longs perform best when a stock is below its 20-day average, not above it (reversion, not momentum).

ATR / volatility gate Market regime gate Volume gate Trend (reversion) gate QQQ divergence carve-out — validated, live Entry-range & freshness gates

Decision-support phase

Manual

Ran a live monitor that surfaced recommendations for manual review and confirmation — the proving ground where the rule set was pressure-tested against real, in-the-moment market behavior and Sebastian's own read of specific setups.

QQQ single-horizon entry filter — validated, kept optional Regime-conditional stop width Multi-horizon QQQ/SOXX confirmation Hybrid per-ticker index filtering CTA pressure-monitor overlay
Postmortems on live misses (e.g. WDC, SNDK) were resolved with evidence from the full trade history, not gut calls — several times confirming a gate's rejection was correct even when it felt wrong in hindsight.

The pivot: full automation

Decision

After weeks of stable, incrementally-improved rules, made the deliberate call to stop manually placing trades and let the system execute them itself — full automation, no manual confirm step, auto-flatten at market close.

Building the execution layer

Shipped

Built a broker-specific automation layer on top of the existing engine: Alpaca bracket orders (entry + stop + target in one submission), position tracking, and a 3:55pm ET auto-flatten to match the backtest's own exit assumptions exactly.

Live bugs, caught and fixed

Resolved

Production use surfaced two real gaps, both fixed the same day they were found. First: auto-execution firing on several days-old, unresolved recommendations against today's prices — fixed with a same-day freshness filter and permanent failure/skip recording so nothing is silently retried. Second: an order rejected by the broker for a stale price wasn't reflected in the audit log — fixed with a pre-submission price-freshness check (bid/ask midpoint, double-confirmed to rule out a single noisy tick) plus full skip/reject logging, so the audit log is now a true record of every recommendation's fate.

Full autonomous scheduling

Live

Removed the last manual step: the system now starts, runs, and stops itself every weekday without anyone opening a terminal. A macOS scheduled service (launchd) fires the monitor at market open, logs the full session to a timestamped file, and pushes a notification on completion or failure — whether or not anyone is at the machine.

Testing generalization beyond tech

Tested

Ran the exact locked strategy — zero refitting, zero new parameter search — against 50 tickers across five non-tech sectors, to check whether the edge was a general market effect or specific to the tech/AI universe it was built on. Result: it does not generalize. Every sector traded net negative, before and after the trend/divergence gates, and the effect was broad-based rather than a few bad names. Valuable evidence against scaling this exact rule set into other sectors — and confirmation that any multi-sector expansion needs its own from-scratch discovery process, not a copy-paste of the tech ruleset.

Financials — net negative Healthcare — net negative Energy — net negative Industrials — net negative Consumer staples — net negative

Today

Running

System runs itself unattended every weekday: scheduled start, live monitoring of all 34 tickers, real-time signal gating, automatic bracket-order execution, and auto-flatten at close — no manual step from open to close.
